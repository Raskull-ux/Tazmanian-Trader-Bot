# Gap-up run day vs dump day, decided by 9:45

Names: QQQ, AAPL, TSLA, NVDA, META, AMD, HOOD. 5m bars with extended hours. Every gap up of 0.3%+ over the prior close: 6,956 days (3,618 in 2016-2021 = fit, 3,338 in 2022-2026 = judged; HOOD from Aug 2021).
**Dump day** = closes red (4:00 close under the 9:30 open). **Run day** = closes green.
Because the first 15 minutes are already inside the day's candle, every clue is also measured **from the 9:45 price**, which is what a trade placed at 9:45 actually gets: did it close under the 9:45 price, and did it fall 0.5 ATR before it rose 0.5 ATR (rest of the session).

## The answer in plain English

**The 9:45 rule:** on a gap up of 0.3%+, if the first 15-minute candle closes back under yesterday's close, it is a dump day: it closed red 77% of the time in 2016-21 and 74% in 2022-26 (baseline 48% / 48%). If instead the first 15m candle is green and its low was the 9:30 print, it is a run day: closed green 71% / 71%.

**The catch, said straight:** most of that is the first 15 minutes already being in the candle. Measured from the 9:45 price, the dump-day rule closed lower than 9:45 only 52% / 49% of the time and fell 0.5 ATR before rising 0.5 ATR 31% / 27% (all gap-ups: 28% / 29%). So the 9:45 read tells you what kind of day it is, but a put bought at the 9:45 price has no real edge to the close on these 7 names. The edge, if any, is in the entry after 9:45 (the pop back into the prior close or the 9:30 high), which this test does not cover.

The best pair found on 2016-21 was **Gap 1-2% + First 15m closed back UNDER the prior close**: closed red 83% (n 65) then 75% (n 103) in 2022-26; from 9:45 it went lower 62% then 42%. Small sample; the judged half did not keep the 9:45 edge.
